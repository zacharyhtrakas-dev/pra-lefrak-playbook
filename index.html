FDNY Fire Alarm Inspection + As-Built Submission
Applies to: B1, B2, A3, A4 — 96-05 HHE
In Progress ▼
FDNY must inspect and approve the fire alarm system before a Letter of Approval (LOA) can be issued, which is required for the DOB Letter of Completion (LOC). The as-built riser diagram must be submitted to the Fire Alarm Inspection Unit before or at the time of inspection. Key insight for B2/A3/A4: because the drawing changes were minor (under 10 devices/floor), a PAA is NOT required — the as-built can reflect the changes and the inspector accepts or rejects at their discretion.
Chain of custody — current situation (B2, A3, A4)

MEP/FA drawings signed by electrical contractor (Troon)
Troon (electrical and FA contractor) reviews the as-built riser diagram for accuracy against actual installation and signs the functionality statement. 👤 Troon (elec/FA contractor)

MEP engineer (MGE) signs and seals as-built riser diagram
MGE (MEP engineers, EOR) must draft, sign, and seal the as-built riser diagram reflecting actual installation. Must be 11"x17", include I/O matrix, functionality statement, title block with all job numbers (PW-1, FPIMS, Record ID). Signed and sealed by the licensed design professional who is applicant of record. Current status B2/A3/A4: Drawings now at MGE for EOR signature.
👤 MGE (EOR)
3
As-built sent to Hi Rise — they call for FDNY inspection
Once as-built is signed and sealed by MGE, it goes to Hi Rise (GC / expeditor). Hi Rise submits the as-built to the Fire Alarm Inspection Unit and calls for the FDNY inspection. Confirm Hi Rise has everything they need before the call is placed.
👤 Hi Rise
4
PAA decision tree — do you need one?
Ask: what changed from the approved plans?
✓ NO PAA needed — as-built only
• Added up to 10 devices/appliances per floor
• Up to 10 devices shown on approved plans were NOT installed
• DGPs installed inconsistent with approved plans
• System performance not downgraded
→ Reflect changes in the signed/sealed as-built. Inspector accepts at their discretion. An affidavit from the applicant of record may be included on the as-built.
⚠ PAA optional — you have a choice
When fewer than 10 devices were added, you can choose to file a PAA anyway for extra certainty — but it's not required. The as-built route is faster. B2/A3/A4 are in this category — we are taking the as-built route (no PAA).
✕ PAA REQUIRED — no exceptions
• Added or removed MORE than 10 devices/appliances on a floor
• Relocated fire alarm control panels or Fire Command Centers
• Changes affecting system type or job description
• Changes affecting work floors
• Changes requiring further review (partial coverage, new floor layouts)
• ANY modification to ARCS (auxiliary radio communication) — always requires PAA

ECC prepares ARTS drawings

DEP reviews drawings

ARTS filing submitted to DOB

Design Change / Drawing Revision Process
Applies to: all DOB-filed work — HHE + JB
Reference ▼
When the actual construction deviates from approved plans, the deviation must be documented and handled correctly. The route depends on the severity of the change. Getting this wrong (e.g. filing a PAA unnecessarily) wastes weeks. Understanding the threshold is critical.
1
Identify the change and assess severity
Document exactly what changed from approved plans. Key questions: How many devices added/removed per floor? Did any control panels move? Did work floors change? Did system type change? Does it require further review (partial coverage, new floor layouts)?
👤 MEP engineer / GC + PRA
2
Route A: Minor change → As-built only (no PAA)
Per FDNY Bulletin 12-11-2023: up to 10 devices/appliances added per floor = as-built only, no further documentation needed. Inspector accepts at discretion. Other minor changes (up to 10 devices not installed, DGPs inconsistent with plans, system performance not downgraded) = affidavit from applicant of record, may appear on signed/sealed as-built.
👤 Design professional (applicant of record)
✓ This is the route for B2, A3, A4. No PAA needed.
3
Route B: Major change → Post-Approval Amendment (PAA)
A PAA IS required for: (a) relocation of fire alarm control panels / Fire Command Centers, (b) changes affecting system type or job description, (c) changes affecting work floors, (d) changes requiring further review, (e) addition/exclusion of MORE than 10 devices per floor. PAA adds weeks to the process and must be filed and approved before inspection.
👤 Design professional → DOB
⚠ ARCS (auxiliary radio communication) modifications always require a PAA — no exceptions.

Substantial Completion + DOB Letter of Completion (LOC)
Applies to: all HHE phases + JB floors at completion
Reference ▼
The DOB Letter of Completion (LOC) is the official DOB sign-off that closes out a construction permit. Substantial Completion (SC) is a contractual milestone between Lefrak and DEP/DCAS that the space is ready for occupancy. Both are required at the end of each phase. Getting to LOC requires all sign-offs to be in hand.
1
All physical work complete — punchlist issued
GC declares substantial completion. DEP/DCAS and PRA do a formal walkthrough and issue a punchlist. All punchlist items must be resolved before sign-offs can be collected.
👤 GC + DEP/DCAS + PRA
2
Collect all required sign-offs
All of the following must be in hand before LOC can be filed: (1) MEP as-builts signed by MGE, (2) Arch as-builts signed by AJA, (3) FDNY Letter of Approval, (4) BEC/Electrical sign-off, (5) Certified air balancing report, (6) DOB plumbing sign-off (plumber signature), (7) FA pretest certification, (8) Emergency lighting test.
👤 MGE + AJA + FDNY + BEC + Testing labs
3
Muhammad (MBC) files for LOC with DOB
Once all sign-offs are collected, Muhammad files the LOC application via DOB NOW. DOB reviews and issues the letter. This is the official DOB record that the project is complete.
👤 Muhammad (MBC) → DOB
4
Substantial Completion letter executed with Lefrak/DEP
Anita Jhamb (Lefrak) issues the Substantial Completion notice. DEP/DCAS signs off. This triggers the contractual start of the warranty period and allows DEP to re-occupy the space.
